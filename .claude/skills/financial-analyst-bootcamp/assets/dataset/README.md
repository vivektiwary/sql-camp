# The SQL Camp practice database

Everything in this database is **made up**. The companies, customers and
vendors do not exist; the prices and financials are generated by formula.
Nothing here is real market data, so you can screenshot it, publish your
queries, and put your work in a portfolio without worrying about anyone's
confidential information.

It is also **deterministic**: the same numbers appear on every machine that
loads it. That matters because it means an answer key can be exact.

Load it with `scripts/setup_db.sh`. Check it with `04_verify.sql`.

---

## The story behind the data

You have joined **Bluewater Capital**, a small investment and advisory firm.
You sit between three teams, which is why the database has three parts:

| Part | Whose data it is | What you use it for |
|---|---|---|
| **Market & statements** | 20 listed companies Bluewater covers | Equity research, valuation, comps, DCF |
| **Our own ledger** | Meridian Softworks Ltd, a portfolio company Bluewater controls | FP&A: budget vs actual, cost control, month-end close |
| **Portfolios** | Three funds Bluewater manages | Position keeping, performance, exposure |

Meridian Softworks appears twice on purpose — once as a listed company
(`company_id = 7`) and once as "our company" whose general ledger we can see
line by line. That lets you do something rare: build a bottom-up analysis
from the ledger and check it against the reported statements.

## Table reference

### Dimensions — the lists of things

| Table | One row per | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `dim_date` | calendar day, 2021–2027 | Fiscal year runs **April–March**. `FY26` = Apr-2025 to Mar-2026. |
| `dim_company` | listed company (20) | India, USA and UK; 10 sectors |
| `dim_account` | ledger account (30) | Our chart of accounts, with `normal_balance` DR/CR |
| `dim_department` | cost centre (8) | Engineering, Sales, Marketing, … |
| `dim_customer` | customer of Meridian (900) | Segment, country, signup date, acquisition channel |

### Market data

| Table | Grain | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `fact_price` | company × trading day | Apr-2023 → Jun-2026, weekdays only. **Market holidays are not removed** — that is a deliberate flaw you will find. |
| `fact_fx_rate` | currency pair × day | USD/GBP/EUR/AED against INR, and the inverses |

### Financial statements — quarterly, FY24-Q1 to FY26-Q4

| Table | Grain |
|---|---|
| `fs_income_statement` | company × quarter |
| `fs_balance_sheet` | company × quarter |
| `fs_cash_flow` | company × quarter |

These three **tie to each other by construction**:

* `total_assets = total_liabilities + total_equity` for every row
* `net_change_cash = cfo + cfi + cff` for every row
* this quarter's cash = last quarter's cash + `net_change_cash`

Amounts are in **millions** of the company's reporting currency.

### Our ledger

| Table | Grain | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `gl_journal_line` | one line of one journal entry | Every `journal_id` has exactly 2 lines and debits = credits |
| `gl_budget` | FY × month × account × department | FY25 and FY26 only |
| `gl_headcount` | FY × month × department | Headcount and average salary |

Amounts are in **rupees**, not millions. Mixing the two up is the single
most common mistake students make with this dataset — and a very common
mistake in real jobs.

Three cost anomalies are planted in the ledger on purpose. Finding them is
an exercise, so they are not listed here.

### Subscription business

| Table | Grain | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `saas_subscription` | customer | `end_date IS NULL` means still a customer |
| `saas_invoice` | invoice | `status` is Paid / Open / Written Off |
| `saas_payment` | payment | Only paid invoices have one |

Data cut-off is **30-Jun-2026**. Anything later has not happened yet.

### Portfolios

| Table | Grain |
|---|---|
| `pf_portfolio` | fund (3) |
| `pf_trade` | trade |
| `pf_holding` | fund × company × quarter-end |

### The messy table

`raw_vendor_invoices` is 40 rows of deliberately awful data: duplicate rows,
amounts stored as text with commas and brackets, three date formats plus an
impossible date, the same vendor spelled four ways, missing currencies and
missing cost centres. Module 12 is about cleaning it with SQL, not by hand.

## Known imperfections (all deliberate)

Real data is never clean. These are here so you learn to spot the shape of a
problem before it embarrasses you in a meeting:

1. **No market holidays.** `fact_price` has every weekday, including days
   the exchange would have been shut.
2. **`raw_vendor_invoices` is a landfill.** By design.
3. **Meridian's ledger and Meridian's reported statements do not reconcile
   exactly.** They are generated by different processes, the way a
   management ledger and a statutory filing genuinely differ. Explaining
   *why* two numbers that "should" match do not is most of an analyst's job.
4. **Zeros and NULLs both appear** where a value is unknown. Deciding which
   is which is part of the work.
